## 1. Path overview content

*Drafted to be used nearly verbatim in the fixture Path overview surface.*

### What this practice is

DJ/VJ and live audiovisual performance is the craft of performing recorded or generated sound and image for a room, in real time. A DJ selects and blends recorded music so a night feels continuous and alive; a VJ performs visuals — video loops, generative graphics, live-coded imagery — in sync with that music; an audiovisual (AV) performer does some combination of both. Practitioners describe the core skill in strikingly similar terms on both sides: it is less about the equipment and more about selection, timing, and reading the room. As one open-decks regular puts it, "95% of being a good DJ is picking good tunes and having fun" ([Pioneer DJ blog](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/how-to-navigate-the-thriving-world-of-open-decks/)).

### What it looks like in real settings

- **Club and bar booths.** The classic setting: warm-up slots, peak-time sets, and the etiquette that goes with them ("it's called the warm up slot for a reason" — [Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/how-to-dj-warm-up-sets/)). Clubs worldwide standardize on the same media-player hardware, and performers show up with a prepared USB stick and headphones rather than their own decks ([ZIPDJ](https://www.zipdj.com/blog/cdj-vs-controller), [Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/help-i-need-to-play-on-cdjs/)).
- **Open decks and open-projector nights.** The scene's open-mic equivalent: sign-up sheets or raffles, short sets, venue-supplied gear, and a strong stay-and-watch-everyone-else norm ([Pioneer DJ blog](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/how-to-navigate-the-thriving-world-of-open-decks/)).
- **Alongside a DJ, at the side of the stage.** Working VJs perform from a table with sightlines to the floor, syncing by tapped tempo to a DJ they often cannot wire into; the community's "Respect Your VJ" manifesto documents both the working conditions to ask for and the duty to deliver ([VJ Union](https://vjun.io/vdmo/respect-your-vj-17ki)).
- **Livestreams.** A streamed short set is the lowest-stakes first outlet, though platform choice is now a licensing decision, not just a technical one (Twitch's DJ Program vs Mixcloud's licensed streaming — [Twitch blog](https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2024/06/06/introducing-the-twitch-dj-program/), [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/longreads/mixclouds-new-live-streaming-platform-gamechanger-djs-%E2%80%93%C2%A0heres-why)).
- **Algoraves and art spaces.** Live-coded music and visuals with the code projected — "Show us your screens" ([TOPLAP](https://toplap.org/wiki/ManifestoDraft)) — plus gallery installations and mapped façades at the projection-mapping edge of the practice.

### Variants

- **DJ-led.** Music performance first; visuals are a supporting layer (BPM-synced loops, or video mixed inside DJ software such as VirtualDJ or Serato Video — [VirtualDJ wiki](https://virtualdj.com/wiki/VideoMixing.html), [Serato](https://serato.com/video)).
- **VJ-led.** Visual performance first, usually clip-based in a layer-grid tool; the performer works alongside DJs and syncs by tap tempo and phrasing ([Resolume BPM manual](https://resolume.com/support/en/bpm)).
- **No-code.** Both of the above are fully practicable without programming: clip decks, knobs, faders, and pre-made or purchased content. This is the fastest documented route to a first gig ("gig-ready in 2–3 months" on the clip route — [VJ Academy](https://vjacademy.info/learn/how-to-become-a-vj)).
- **Generative / live-coded.** Building your own instrument in a node-based environment (TouchDesigner — "a commitment, not a quick start" — [VJ Academy](https://vjacademy.info/learn/best-vj-software)) or live-coding visuals and patterns in the browser (Hydra, Strudel — free, zero-install — [hydra docs](https://hydra.ojack.xyz/docs/), [strudel.cc](https://strudel.cc/learn/getting-started/)).

### Time, cost, equipment, and access realities

- **Time.** A first beatmatched blend is possible in an afternoon; a clean home set takes 1–3 months of consistent practice; first real gigs commonly arrive at 6–12 months; club residencies at 2–4 years ([Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/learn-to-dj-the-complete-beginners-guide/), [The Ghost Production](https://theghostproduction.com/dj-resources/how-long-to-learn-dj/), [DJs On Demand](https://www.djsondemand.co.uk/how-long-does-it-take-to-become-a-dj/)). On the visual side, the clip route can be gig-ready in 2–3 months; the generative route is measured in months before it is stage-ready ([VJ Academy](https://vjacademy.info/learn/how-to-become-a-vj)). Consistency beats session length in every account.
- **Cost.** Genuine $0 starts exist on both sides: free open-source DJ software ([Mixxx](https://www.lexicondj.com/blog/free-dj-software)), an indefinite watermarked VJ-software trial ([Resolume forum](https://resolume.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=21240)), a free non-commercial generative license ([Derivative](https://docs.derivative.ca/TouchDesigner_Products)), and free Creative-Commons loop libraries ([Beeple](https://www.beeple-crap.com/vjloops)). The standard first purchase on the DJ side is a ~$329 two-channel controller ([Sweetwater](https://www.sweetwater.com/store/detail/DDJFLX4--pioneer-dj-ddj-flx4-2-deck-rekordbox-and-serato-dj-controller-graphite)); on the VJ side it is a laptop with a dedicated mid-range GPU ([Resolume forum](https://resolume.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=20372)). The hidden ongoing cost for DJs is music itself — $1.49–$2.49/track or $10–35/month pools and streaming ([Mixmag Asia](https://mixmag.asia/read/beatport-raises-prices-up-to-20-percent-starting-august-14-tech), [Digital DJ Pool](https://digitaldjpool.com/pricing/)); for VJs it is mostly time spent making content. Full numbers are in the Realities appendix (§8).
- **Equipment and access.** Nobody buys the club booth: club-standard players cost ~$2,549 each and every serious venue already has them ([RK Audio](https://www.rkaudio.com/pioneer-cdj-3000-and-djm-a9-flagship-dj-system-bundle.html), [ZIPDJ](https://www.zipdj.com/blog/cdj-vs-controller)). Access is the real problem, and it has real solutions: hourly rehearsal rooms with club-standard booths from £8.90/h in London or $16.30/h in New York ([Pirate](https://pirate.com/en/dj-studios/london/)), open-decks nights, asking a venue for pre-opening practice time ([Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/help-i-need-to-play-on-cdjs/)), and VR practice simulators ([TribeXR](https://www.tribexr.com/pricing)). VJs similarly do not buy projectors for gigs — many venues have screens and no VJ, and larger shows rent projection and LED ([chameleonic](https://chameleonic-design.com/top-5-vj-ing-tips/)). What both actually carry is cables, adapters, and backups — "prepare for the worst and hope for the best" ([VDMX travel kit](https://vdmx.vidvox.net/tutorials/vj-travel-kit-whats-in-our-bag)).
- **Live-setting realities.** Venue gear differs from home setups and is not always in good condition ([Pioneer DJ blog](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/how-to-navigate-the-thriving-world-of-open-decks/)); nerves peak in the first 15–20 minutes and pass ([Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/your-first-dj-gig/)); the documented failure modes are unfamiliar hardware and missing backups on the DJ side, and cable/handshake/resolution failures on the visual side ([Ableton forum](https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?t=186643), [XGIMI](https://us.xgimi.com/blogs/projectors-101/why-projector-showing-no-signal-cables-connected)). Audiences reliably notice selection and energy, not fluffed transitions ([Challenge Fifty Two](https://www.challengefiftytwo.com/played-open-decks/)).

### Common foundations and genuine alternatives

Nearly every real curriculum — commercial DJ schools, official VJ-software training, generative bootcamps — converges on the same small set of foundations: rhythm and song structure (phrasing), knowing your material cold, signal flow, and performing for real people early and often ([Digital DJ Tips course](https://1.digitaldjtips.com/complete-dj-course-info), [Resolume training](https://www.resolume.com/training), [Scratch DJ Academy](https://academy.scratch.com/courses/dj-certification)). Almost everything else is genuinely contested: whether to learn manual beatmatching before using sync, whether music theory helps at all, whether visuals should react automatically to audio or be performed by hand, whether to start on cheap gear or club-standard gear, and whether to use stock content or make everything yourself. These are documented, ongoing disagreements among named practitioners — different Guides through this Path take different sides, and that is expected.

### Neighboring Paths

Four Paths border this one in the Atlas, sharing Nodes without sharing an identity. **Music Production** is the closest neighbor — "DJ/producer" is effectively one hyphenated career in dance music, sharing rhythm/structure, key knowledge, and library craft, but it is a studio practice measured in finished tracks rather than live sets. **Creative Coding for Music** generates sound and image from live-written code instead of mixing recordings — free toolchain, browser entry, the algorave scene. **Projection Mapping** fits video to architecture and objects — site- and geometry-driven rather than beat-driven. **Event Technology** is the crew-side trade (sound, lighting, staging) that overlaps most on signal-flow craft and differs most in its apprenticeship-shaped route. Full stub descriptions for all four are in §6.

## 2. Proposed shared Nodes

Seventeen Nodes — one over the plan's "approximately 12–16," counted honestly: the plan itself lists the private one-track experiment and the ten-minute set as separate items, and §9.3 explains why further merging would hide real granularity. Role columns show each Node's route role in **Guide A** (Club-first DJ/VJ with borrowed gear) and **Guide B** (Visual-first reactive performance). Each is grounded in the curricula research; the "recurring teachable units" survey found roughly the same 12–16 units across every curriculum studied, with Guides differing mostly in order and emphasis — which is exactly the shared-Atlas premise (H3).

**Which real curricula teach each Node:**

- **Rhythm & Song Structure** — every curriculum studied: Digital DJ Tips Mixing Part 1 ("music structure, counting & timing"), Scratch DJ Academy Level 1 ("basic music theory & song structure"), Udemy's "beats & bars, phrasing," Resolume's official Chapter 4 "Rhythm," and Steve Kislov's VJ version ("Not Playing On The Beat" as a top-three beginner mistake — [StvInMotion](https://www.stvinmotion.com/three-beginner-vj-mistakes/)). Phrasing is "the difference between sounding like a beginner and a pro" ([LearningToDJ](https://learningtodj.com/blog/ultimate-guide-to-phrasing-in-djing/)).
- **Music Selection & Library** — Digital DJ Tips Module 3 (before mixing); LSA's beginner course puts library prep before touching the decks; Digital DJ Pool's beginner-mistakes canon ("you can't learn track selection with nothing to select from" — [Digital DJ Pool](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/dj-mistakes-beginners-make/)). Note the ordering disagreement: the Udemy bestseller teaches it *after* technique ([Udemy](https://www.udemy.com/course/l2dj-btoi/)).
- **Music Theory Fundamentals** — taught explicitly by Point Blank (Module 2 pairs DJing with Ableton production), the Udemy course (Section 2), and recommended by Chris M ([Hot Cue DJ](https://reallychrism.substack.com/p/20-years-of-dj-advice-in-1-email)); explicitly disclaimed as necessary by Digital DJ Tips ([Phil Morse](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/why-you-dont-have-to-be-a-musician-to-be-a-good-dj/)).
- **Playing an Instrument** — recommended by Chris M; treated as irrelevant by Phil Morse; the turntable-as-instrument position is QBert's ([RBMA](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2019/01/qbert-interview/)); structured pad training exists ([Melodics](https://melodics.com/finger-drumming)). No curriculum requires it.
- **Harmonic Mixing** — beginner material at Crossfader and LSA ("harmonic mixing" in lesson one's library prep); "Masterclass"-tier in Broughton & Brewster's *How to DJ (Properly)* ([Penguin](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/333746/how-to-dj-properly-by-frank-broughton-and-bill-brewster/9780593058114)); "Advanced" in the Udemy course. The Camelot system is marketed precisely as theory-free ([Mixed In Key](https://mixedinkey.com/harmonic-mixing-guide/), [Lexicon](https://www.lexicondj.com/blog/the-camelot-wheel-explained-harmonic-mixing-without-the-music-theory)).
- **Mixing Technique** — the spine of every DJ curriculum: Crossfader (beatmatch-first), Digital DJ Tips Mixing Parts 1–2, Point Blank, Scratch DJ Academy (manual timing from Level 1), LSA (jog-wheel beatmatching → EQ blending → phrasing → FX).
- **Signal Flow & Rig Setup** — Digital DJ Tips Module 2 ("the four things needed to play on any setup"); live-sound pedagogy's core concept ([Pro Audio Files](https://theproaudiofiles.com/complete-guide-basics-live-sound/)); the VJ travel-kit canon ([VDMX](https://vdmx.vidvox.net/tutorials/vj-travel-kit-whats-in-our-bag)).
- **Gear Access & Practice Setup** — Crossfader's gear tiers; Turntable Trader's arbitration ("buy the unit that gets you mixing this month" — [Turntable Trader](https://turntabletrader.com/blogs/news/cdjs-vs-controllers-for-beginners)); Pirate rental rooms ([pirate.com](https://pirate.com/en/dj-studios/london/)); [TribeXR](https://www.tribexr.com/pricing); Digital DJ Tips' ask-the-venue advice.
- **Visual Composition** — Resolume official training Chapters 2–3 (Compositing, Color); VJ Academy's Resolume sequence (Layers → Blend Modes → Masking); Gray Area's TouchDesigner weeks on compositing and materials ([Gray Area](https://grayarea.org/course/touchdesigner-101/)).
- **Visual Content Library & Prep** — VJ Academy (Codec Conversion lesson); Ghosteam's loop-creation course ([ghosteaminc.com](https://www.ghosteaminc.com/vjblog/create-vj-loops-on-your-own-in-5-steps/)); the Beeple debate ([Zero To VJ](https://zerotovj.com/should-you-stop-vjing-with-beeple-clips/)); loop conventions ([VJ Galaxy](https://vjgalaxy.com/blogs/resources-digital-assets/what-are-vj-loops-and-how-to-download-them)).
- **Reactive Visuals** — taught as *two separate units* in Resolume's own training ([4.2 BPM Sync](https://resolume.com/training/1/4/24), [4.5 Audio Reactive](https://www.resolume.com/training/2/11/67)); TouchDesigner's audioAnalysis pedagogy including 64-beat "music math" ([IIHQ](https://interactiveimmersive.io/blog/deployment/creating-dj-visuals-with-touchdesigner/)); IIHQ Bootcamp project 1 is literally "Audio-Reactive Live Music Content" ([IIHQ Bootcamp](https://interactiveimmersive.io/touchdesigner-bootcamp/)).
- **Projection & Display Basics** — Resolume training Chapter 7; VJ Academy's Advanced Output / Display Tips lessons; Gray Area week 12; rider-level realities ([Superview TV](https://www.superviewtv.com/rider/), [LED Nation USA](https://lednationusa.com/setting-up-a-vjs-booth-with-an-led-screen/)).
- **Live Control Surfaces** — Resolume training Chapter 6 (MIDI, OSC); controller-by-style guidance ([LIME ART GROUP](https://limeartgroup.com/best-midi-controllers-of-vjs-visual-artists/), [Resolume forum](https://resolume.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=13346)); jog/pitch technique across DJ schools.
- **Club Media-Player Workflow** — Crossfader's CDJ/XDJ Masterclass; Digital DJ Tips' controller-to-CDJ guide ("preparation becomes more important" — [DDT](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/complete-guide-to-switching-from-controllers-to-cdjs/)); LSA/Point Blank teach on club players from lesson one.
- **Observing a Live Set** — ZIPDJ's Boiler-Room-as-study-material guidance ([ZIPDJ](https://www.zipdj.com/blog/boiler-room-dj)); open-decks stay-and-watch etiquette ([Pioneer DJ blog](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/how-to-navigate-the-thriving-world-of-open-decks/)); "go see other people's visuals" ([chameleonic](https://chameleonic-design.com/top-5-vj-ing-tips/)).
- **Private One-Track Experiment / Ten-Minute AV Set** — Crossfader's "press play on your first mix" and "you are DJing!" milestones; Scratch DJ Academy's assessed 10-minute performance set at Level 2 ([Scratch](https://academy.scratch.com/courses/dj-certification)); the synthesized anatomy of a first 10-minute AV set (3–4 tracks, one deck of 10–20 loops cut to 2/4/8-beat events, tap-tempo sync, fallback loop) from practitioner guidance ([Resolume BPM manual](https://resolume.com/support/en/bpm), [Challenge Fifty Two](https://www.challengefiftytwo.com/played-open-decks/)).

**Provisional custom-Node candidates (deliberately left out of the shared seventeen):** the plan's acceptance criteria require a Creator to create a visibly provisional custom Node during the session, so the fixture needs plausible material for that moment. Two research-grounded candidates: **Open-Decks Etiquette** — sign-up norms, staying for other performers' sets, leaving the booth as you found it ([Pioneer DJ blog](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/how-to-navigate-the-thriving-world-of-open-decks/)) — and **Tap-Tempo Drill** — practicing beat-tapping against recorded DJ sets until triggered visuals land on phrase (implied by the [Resolume BPM workflow](https://resolume.com/support/en/bpm) and Kislov's anticipation pedagogy). Both are real and teachable but too narrow for the shared Atlas — exactly the shape a provisional Node should have.

## 3. Guide A: Club-first DJ/VJ with borrowed gear

### Creator persona

A working club DJ who came up through open-decks nights and warm-up slots, modeled on the club-gear-first school position (London Sound Academy / Point Blank teach beginners on club-standard players from lesson one — [LSA](https://www.londonsoundacademy.com/courses/beginner-dj-course), [Point Blank](https://www.pointblankmusicschool.com/courses/la/dj-courses/dj-skills-level-1/)) combined with the ear-first, theory-skeptic stance ([Phil Morse](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/why-you-dont-have-to-be-a-musician-to-be-a-good-dj/)).

- **Audience:** adults in or near a city with rental studios and open-decks nights, on a limited budget, who want to play for real people in real rooms rather than perfect a bedroom setup.
- **Starting point:** a laptop and headphones; no music training assumed; no gear ownership assumed.
- **Outcome:** perform a ten-minute audiovisual set on club-standard gear at an open-decks night — realistically within 6–12 months of consistent practice ([The Ghost Production](https://theghostproduction.com/dj-resources/how-long-to-learn-dj/)).
- **Practical assumptions:** clubs provide the players — you bring a USB stick and headphones ([Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/help-i-need-to-play-on-cdjs/)); hourly club-standard practice rooms exist near you (£8.90–£12.10/h London, $16.30–$24.20/h NYC/LA — [Pirate](https://pirate.com/en/dj-studios/london/)); visuals are a supporting layer, run as BPM-synced loops on whatever screen the venue has, using free-tier software ([Resolume forum](https://resolume.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=21240)).

### Ordered branching route

1. **Rhythm & Song Structure** — *required.* "Mixing happens at phrase boundaries; learn to count before you learn to touch anything" ([LearningToDJ](https://learningtodj.com/blog/ultimate-guide-to-phrasing-in-djing/)).
2. **Music Selection & Library Building** — *required, deliberately before technique.* Mirrors LSA's syllabus (library prep before the decks) and the beginner-mistakes canon: practicing on fifteen songs is the classic motivation-killer ([Digital DJ Pool](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/dj-mistakes-beginners-make/)).
3. **Signal Flow & Rig Setup** — *required.* The four things needed to play on any setup; venue gear varies and is not always in industry-standard condition ([DDT](https://1.digitaldjtips.com/complete-dj-course-info), [Pioneer DJ blog](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/how-to-navigate-the-thriving-world-of-open-decks/)).
4. **Gear Access & Practice Setup** — *required, with the Guide's* ***alternative branch***:
   - **Main route: rent the club booth by the hour.** Practice on the same players you will meet at gigs (Pirate rooms from £8.90/h), plus open decks and asking venues for pre-opening time ([Pirate](https://pirate.com/en/dj-studios/london/), [DDT](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/help-i-need-to-play-on-cdjs/)).
   - **Alternative: own a starter controller** (~$329 street; ultra-budget under £100; used predecessor ~$200–300) and bridge to club players later via Step 5 ([Sweetwater](https://www.sweetwater.com/store/detail/DDJFLX4--pioneer-dj-ddj-flx4-2-deck-rekordbox-and-serato-dj-controller-graphite), [Zoundhouse](https://www.zoundhouse.de/en/blog/pioneer-dj-ddj-flx4-vs-ddj-400-flx2-which-controller-is-the-best)).
   - **Alternative: free software, keyboard only** — open-source DJ software on the laptop you already own; a real learning stopgap, not a destination ([Lexicon](https://www.lexicondj.com/blog/free-dj-software)). *(This is the branch the Explorer journey's "keyboard-only" remix maps onto.)*
5. **Mixing Technique (Tempo, EQ & Blends)** — *required.* Learn manual beatmatching by ear even though sync exists, because club players don't show side-by-side waveforms and beatgrids fail; then use sync as a tool ([DJ TechTools](https://djtechtools.com/2017/07/23/ddj-cdjs-practicing-cdjs-pioneer-dj-controller/), [DJ Mentors](https://www.djmentors.com/p/the-sync-button)).
6. **Club Media-Player Workflow** — *required.* On this route it is a hard prerequisite: only exported tracks exist in the booth; two FAT32 sticks, always ([DDT](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/complete-guide-to-switching-from-controllers-to-cdjs/)).
7. **Harmonic Mixing (Key Awareness)** — *recommended.* Use the Camelot wheel and key tags as a servant, not a master; the perfect next track may not be in a compatible key ([DJ TechTools](https://djtechtools.com/2010/03/23/to-key-or-not-to-key/)).
8. **Reactive Visuals** — *recommended.* One deck of loops, tap-tempo synced, on the free trial tier; content cut to 2/4/8-beat events so beat-synced playback lands on phrase ([Resolume BPM manual](https://resolume.com/support/en/bpm)).
9. **Live Control Surfaces** — *recommended.* Hot cues, loops, and one small controller mapped for the visual layer.
10. **Observing a Live Set** — *recommended.* Close-camera streams for phrase mixing and crowd-reading; attend open decks alone and stay for everyone's sets ([ZIPDJ](https://www.zipdj.com/blog/boiler-room-dj), [Pioneer DJ blog](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/how-to-navigate-the-thriving-world-of-open-decks/)).
11. **Private One-Track Experiment** — *recommended.* Record one blend, listen back; recording reveals "where your weaknesses are" ([Milkbar Nick](https://milkbarnick.substack.com/p/from-dj-nixperience-to-milkbar-nick)).
12. **Ten-Minute Audiovisual Set** — ***checkpoint.*** 3–4 pre-planned tracks with rehearsed transitions at an open-decks night, visuals on tap-tempo, backup USB in your pocket. Pre-planning a first set is explicitly endorsed for beginners ([Challenge Fifty Two](https://www.challengefiftytwo.com/played-open-decks/)).

*Optional depth:* **Music Theory Fundamentals**, **Playing an Instrument**, **Visual Composition**, **Visual Content Library & Prep**, **Projection & Display Basics**.

*A note on the plan's "music selection and live control" emphasis:* in this Guide the live-control craft lives in the required **Mixing Technique** and **Club Media-Player Workflow** Nodes (jog wheels, pitch faders, hot cues on club players) — the separate **Live Control Surfaces** Node is *recommended* because on this route it covers only the supplementary visual layer.

### The Creator's stated rationale for making theory and instrument practice optional

> "Nothing in this route requires reading music or playing keys. You need musical passion, but 'do DJs need to play instruments? Nope' — that's not me, that's the founder of the biggest online DJ school, a decade-long Manchester club resident who has played the main room at Privilege, Ibiza ([Phil Morse](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/why-you-dont-have-to-be-a-musician-to-be-a-good-dj/)). The one piece of theory you cannot skip is already Step 1: phrasing and song structure. Key mixing is covered by the Camelot wheel, which was built so you never have to know that 8A is A minor ([Lexicon](https://www.lexicondj.com/blog/the-camelot-wheel-explained-harmonic-mixing-without-the-music-theory)) — and some of the best DJs warn against being 'a slave to this particular readout' anyway ([Objekt, via MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/harmonic-mixing)). Your ears are always king in the end ([Butch, via Pioneer DJ](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/how-do-djs-approach-harmonic-mixing/)). I keep both Nodes visible as optional depth rather than deleting them, because they genuinely pay off for two kinds of people: anyone heading toward production — where theory becomes effectively mandatory even by my source's own admission — and anyone who finds ear training through an instrument makes them faster. Other Guides mark these core, and they have their reasons. Read both and decide."

Every claim above traces to §5's documented positions; the Guide surfaces the disagreement instead of hiding it (plan requirement, H2/H3).

## 4. Guide B: Visual-first reactive performance

### Creator persona

A VJ/creative technologist who entered through motion design and generative tools, modeled on the visual-first route documented by VJ Union ("After Effects, Cinema 4D, Blender" for making; performance tools for playing — [VJ Union guide](https://vjun.io/vdmo/how-to-become-a-visual-performer-or-vj-a-comprehensive-guide-f03)) and the free-education TouchDesigner scene ([elekktronaut](https://www.elekktronaut.com/tutorials/touchdesigner-beginner-course), [IIHQ](https://interactiveimmersive.io/touchdesigner-tutorial/)).

- **Audience:** visually-minded adults — designers, coders, generative-art-curious — who want to perform live *images*, usually alongside DJs, and who may have no music background at all.
- **Starting point:** a laptop with a dedicated mid-range GPU (or willingness to get a used gaming laptop); comfort learning visual software; $0 software budget assumed viable.
- **Outcome:** perform a reactive visual set alongside a DJ at a real event — realistically gig-ready in 2–3 months on the clip route, longer on the generative branch ([VJ Academy](https://vjacademy.info/learn/how-to-become-a-vj)).
- **Practical assumptions:** you practice at home to recorded DJ sets (no rental rooms, no music purchases); venues often have screens with no VJ — a 60–90-second reel and an offer to do a first night free gets you in the room ([chameleonic](https://chameleonic-design.com/top-5-vj-ing-tips/), [Resolume forum](https://resolume.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=21263)); reliability matters more than talent at the start; know the "Respect Your VJ" norms before you take paid work ([VJ Union](https://vjun.io/vdmo/respect-your-vj-17ki)).

### Ordered branching route

1. **Visual Composition** — *required.* Layers, blend modes, color, restraint — the craft that separates a performed set from a screensaver (Resolume training Chapters 2–3).
2. **Rhythm & Song Structure** — *required.* Not theory — structure literacy: "mostly 4 beats in every bar and 4 to 8 bars in every phrase… when you know what's coming next, you can prepare the right visuals and trigger them at the right moment" ([Steve Kislov](https://www.stvinmotion.com/three-beginner-vj-mistakes/)). Not playing on the beat is a top-three beginner VJ mistake.
3. **Visual Content Library & Preparation** — *required.* Start with free Creative-Commons loops ([Beeple](https://www.beeple-crap.com/vjloops)) and remix them with effects until your own content exists — with the honest caveat that "everyone uses them" ([Zero To VJ](https://zerotovj.com/should-you-stop-vjing-with-beeple-clips/)); loops cut to 2/4/8-beat events; codec conversion for smooth playback.
4. **Reactive Visuals (Beat Sync & Audio Analysis)** — *required.* Both mechanisms as separate skills: tap-tempo/BPM sync (the working VJ's workhorse for syncing to a DJ you can't wire into) and FFT-driven parameters — plus the author's stance in the automation debate: reactivity is a layer under a performed set, not a replacement for the performer ([Resolume BPM manual](https://resolume.com/support/en/bpm), [CHI 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3313831.3376463)).
5. **Signal Flow & Rig Setup** — *required.* The second video output nobody warns you about, and the adapter tax: quality cables, active adapters, a laptop-free backup loop player ([LIME ART GROUP](https://limeartgroup.com/best-midi-controllers-of-vjs-visual-artists/), [VDMX travel kit](https://vdmx.vidvox.net/tutorials/vj-travel-kit-whats-in-our-bag)).
6. **Projection & Display Basics** — *required.* Ask before every gig: exact LED-wall pixel resolution, HDMI or SDI, where you physically stand ([Superview TV](https://www.superviewtv.com/rider/)). Untested dongles are the documented way first VJ sets die ([Ableton forum](https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?t=186643)).
7. **Live Control Surfaces** — *required.* Map a clip-grid controller; the de facto standard grid controller mirrors the layer/column layout and another VJ on the bill probably has one if yours dies ([LIME ART GROUP](https://limeartgroup.com/best-midi-controllers-of-vjs-visual-artists/)).
8. **Observing a Live Set** — *recommended.* See what other people do with visuals — generative art, 3D, lighting — not just other VJs' clip packs ([chameleonic](https://chameleonic-design.com/top-5-vj-ing-tips/)).
9. **Private One-Track Experiment** — ***checkpoint.*** Map two visual changes to one song section of a recorded set, at home, private. (This is the vocabulary doc's own Quest example, grounded here in Kislov's anticipation pedagogy.)
10. **Ten-Minute Audiovisual Set** — *required (final milestone).* One deck of 10–20 loops in 2–3 energy tiers, tap-tempo synced, one audio-reactive layer, a pre-rendered fallback loop — performed alongside a DJ or streamed.

*Also placed (reconciling the §2 role table):* **Gear Access & Practice Setup** — *recommended,* sitting alongside the starting-point assumption rather than on the spine: checking an existing laptop against the software's GPU guidance, the used-gaming-laptop market, and a TV or borrowed projector for home practice ([Resolume forum](https://resolume.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=20372)). **Music Selection & Library Building** — *optional depth,* surfaced inside the DJ-technique alternative branch below: only Explorers who take that branch need a music library of their own.

**Alternative branch — DJ Technique:** **Mixing Technique (Tempo, EQ & Blends)** is placed as an *alternative* to Step 7's controller-performance focus, for Explorers who want to drive the audio too (the one-laptop video-DJ shape: DJ software that mixes audio and video natively — [VirtualDJ wiki](https://virtualdj.com/wiki/VideoMixing.html), [Serato Video](https://serato.com/video)) — accepting the documented stability tradeoff of one machine doing everything ("I don't have to have four things running on one laptop and then start worrying about the stability" — Jess Jacobs, [Ableton](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/extending-live-how-three-different-artists-approach-visuals-live-performance/)). *Within* the main route, the author also notes the generative variant (node-based tool, free non-commercial license, months-not-weeks learning curve) as a slower alternative entry to Steps 3–4 ([IIHQ comparison](https://interactiveimmersive.io/blog/technology/resolume-vs-touchdesigner/)).

**Excluded/absent Nodes, with the author's reasons:** **Music Theory Fundamentals** — *excluded:* "no VJ source demands formal theory; what you need is Step 2, structure literacy, and that's the whole of it" (grounded in the finding that the VJ-side version of the theory debate is "musicality yes, theory no"). **Playing an Instrument** — *excluded:* "instrument hours serve the audio craft this route does not touch; if you want trained hands, spend those hours on your controller — performed visuals are their own instrument" (the author's counterpart to §5's QBert deck-as-instrument position, applied to the clip grid; the ear-training case for instruments belongs to Guides that teach mixing). **Harmonic Mixing** and **Club Media-Player Workflow** — *absent:* they belong to the audio craft this route doesn't touch.

### At least three material differences from Guide A

1. **Audience:** visually-minded designers/coders possibly with zero music background, vs. music-first club aspirants in Guide A.
2. **Practical assumptions:** home practice on an owned GPU laptop, $0 software, venues-have-screens pitch economics — vs. Guide A's rented club booths, music-purchase economics, and clubs-provide-the-players USB workflow.
3. **Outcome:** a supporting-performer gig alongside a DJ (reel → free first night → paid slots at $400+ under Respect-Your-VJ norms), on a 2–3-month clip-route timeline — vs. Guide A's own ten-minute music set at open decks on a 6–12-month timeline.
4. **Role assignments, not just order:** theory *excluded* here vs *optional depth* there; DJ technique *alternative* here vs *required* there; content-library and projection Nodes *required* here vs *optional depth* there.

## 5. The theory/instrument disagreement

*Written so the fixture can present it as authorial disagreement between Guides, per the plan's requirement to expose rather than settle it.*

### The strongest honest version of "you don't need theory"

The tools were built so you don't. Mixed In Key markets its Camelot system with "You don't even need an understanding of keys or music theory" ([Mixed In Key](https://mixedinkey.com/harmonic-mixing-guide/)), and Lexicon's explainer is blunter: "Harmonic mixing is not music theory. It is a clock, three moves, and clean key tags. You never have to know that 8A happens to be A minor" ([Lexicon](https://www.lexicondj.com/blog/the-camelot-wheel-explained-harmonic-mixing-without-the-music-theory)). Phil Morse — founder of Digital DJ Tips, the largest online DJ school, a decade-long resident at Manchester's Tangled who played the main room at Privilege, Ibiza — states the position outright: DJs of every kind don't *need* to play instruments; musical and non-musical DJs both develop "an uncanny feel for what 'goes'" ([Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/why-you-dont-have-to-be-a-musician-to-be-a-good-dj/)). Celebrated practitioners publicly disclaim training — deadmau5 is "not a pianist" ([MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/classes/deadmau5-teaches-electronic-music-production/chapters/the-deadmau5-process-theory)); Avicii found it "awesome… to make music without the ability" to play ([Quotefancy](https://quotefancy.com/quote/1643310/)). And the sharpest version isn't merely "theory is unnecessary" but "key-obedience actively harms sets": ArielleNYC — "trying to mix in key was stifling my creativity… making my blends boring"; Objekt — "it's more interesting to learn techniques to mix out of key records than to be a slave to this particular readout" ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/harmonic-mixing)). Even at the top, Armin van Buuren key-mixes only occasionally ([DJ TechTools](https://djtechtools.com/2010/03/23/to-key-or-not-to-key/)), and key-tool users like Butch still say "your ears are always king in the end" ([Pioneer DJ blog](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/how-do-djs-approach-harmonic-mixing/)).

### The strongest honest version of "theory and instrument skills help"

David Guetta, in the same Pioneer DJ roundup: "Harmonic mixing is absolutely essential in my opinion"; Erol Alkan "couldn't imagine not being able to use it"; Flava D says key clash "can really make or break a transition" ([Pioneer DJ blog](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/how-do-djs-approach-harmonic-mixing/)). Working-DJ educator Chris M (20+ years): "Learn a musical instrument. It will help train your ear and co-ordination and will improve your DJing in ways you can't imagine" and "Understand at least basic music theory. Your track selections and harmonic mixing will improve" ([Hot Cue DJ](https://reallychrism.substack.com/p/20-years-of-dj-advice-in-1-email)). Zedd — classically trained pianist, ten years a drummer — is the standing example of theory as competitive advantage, and is dismissive of theory-free EDM: "a distorted kick with just four notes on top is not that exciting" ([Sonicbids](https://blog.sonicbids.com/case-study-on-zedd-the-equation-for-success-in-music), [TIDAL](https://tidal.com/magazine/article/zedd-show-you-my-true-colors-uk/1-13923)). And there is a hard technical fact on this side: software key tags are wrong often enough that blind trust fails audibly — best-in-class detection scored ~89% in 2026 testing, free tools 76%, one major store's own metadata ~60% ([OpenKeyScan](https://www.openkeyscan.com/best-key-detection-software-2026); earlier lab test: [DJ TechTools 2015](https://djtechtools.com/amp/2015/11/16/key-detection-software-comparison-2015-edition/)). Even the tool-first route eventually requires trained ears — a point the other camp concedes (Sef Kombo's train-ears-first-then-verify hybrid, same Pioneer piece).

### What both sides accept, and where the honest boundaries are

- **Phrasing and song structure are non-negotiable** — the one "theory" every source teaches, on both the DJ and VJ sides ([LearningToDJ](https://learningtodj.com/blog/ultimate-guide-to-phrasing-in-djing/), [StvInMotion](https://www.stvinmotion.com/three-beginner-vj-mistakes/)). A DJ TechTools forum thread titled "Music Theory 101" turns out to be about structure/arrangement — even practitioners who dismiss "theory" teach a subset of it under another name ([DJTT forum](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/music-theory-101/49058)).
- **The disagreement is about scope, not facts.** Phil Morse himself concedes that production ambitions make "some music theory understanding" effectively mandatory — which is precisely Zedd's case from the other direction.
- **It's genre-dependent.** Plastician: key mixing is essential "if you blend melodic music together"; nearly irrelevant "if you are chopping / dropping stuff" ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/harmonic-mixing)). Guides for different genres may legitimately assign different roles.
- **Nobody argues instrument skills hurt.** The sourced range runs from irrelevant (Morse) to transformative (Chris M, Zedd), with a third position that the deck *is* the instrument (QBert: "It takes time to develop it into [people] knowing that it *is* a musical instrument" — [RBMA](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2019/01/qbert-interview/)) and a modern bridge in structured pad/finger-drumming practice ([Melodics](https://melodics.com/finger-drumming), [Pioneer DJ blog](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/dj-culture/your-guide-to-the-fast-growing-world-of-finger-drumming/)).
- **The VJ mirror is compressed:** musicality yes, theory no — no VJ source demands formal theory, but playing off-beat is a canonical beginner mistake ([StvInMotion](https://www.stvinmotion.com/three-beginner-vj-mistakes/)); and the audio-reactive-automation vs performed-visuals split is the VJ world's sync-button debate ([Zero To VJ](https://zerotovj.com/should-you-use-audio-reactive-visuals/), [CHI 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3313831.3376463)).
- **Cost asymmetry:** theory costs $0 in money and real hours in time; the key-tool shortcut costs ~$58 one-time ([We Are Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/mixed-in-key-11/)); free detection tools exist with lower accuracy. The debate is really about where finite practice hours go.

**Fixture framing:** Guide A marks Music Theory *optional depth* and Playing an Instrument *optional depth* with the Morse/Objekt/Butch rationale; Guide B marks both *excluded* with stated reasons (structure literacy suffices; controller hours beat instrument hours on this route). Both contested Nodes thus carry an explicit, comparable authorial stance in both Guides. One stated limitation: both fixture Guides sit on the skeptical side of the debate, so the live comparison Explorers see is "keep it visible as optional depth" vs "deliberately leave it out" — the pro-theory position (the Guetta/Zedd/Chris M stance, which would mark theory *required*) exists in this fixture set only inside the rationale text and as a hypothetical third producer-DJ Guide. That is permitted by the plan's Guide specs, but Creator sessions should note the full documented dispute is wider than the two fixtures span. The prototype needs only to show that the shared Nodes support all of these assignments without the Atlas taking a side.

## 6. Related Path stubs

**Creative Coding for Music (Live Coding / Algorave).** Performing music and visuals by writing and editing code live, code projected — the TOPLAP scene's founding ethic is "Obscurantism is dangerous. Show us your screens" ([TOPLAP](https://toplap.org/wiki/ManifestoDraft)), and its dance format, the algorave, has run since London 2012 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorave)). The entry point is uniquely frictionless: open a browser-based pattern or video-synth environment ([Strudel](https://strudel.cc/learn/getting-started/), [Hydra](https://hydra.ojack.xyz/docs/)) and make sound or image in under a minute, then graduate to deeper stacks (Sonic Pi; the full pattern-language-plus-synthesis rig whose own docs admit the install "can be difficult" — [TidalCycles](https://tidalcycles.org/docs/getting-started/windows_install/)). Shared Nodes with DJ/VJ: **Rhythm & Song Structure** (the pattern model is cyclic), **Reactive Visuals** (the live-coded video synth is itself a lightweight VJ tool), **Live Control Surfaces**, **Observing a Live Set**, and the performance milestones. It is a different Path because material is *generated from algorithms rather than mixed from recordings*, transparency inverts club convention, the skill floor is text/code fluency rather than beatmatching — and the economics invert too: the entire toolchain is free while DJ/VJ is gear- and license-heavy.

**Projection Mapping.** Warping and masking video to fit real three-dimensional surfaces — façades, stage sets, sculptures — rather than flat screens. Entry: one mid-brightness projector, a textured wall or cardboard sculpture at home, and a beginner-friendly mapping tool's trial; a realistic first setup lands around $650–$1,200 ([BenQ](https://www.benq.com/en-us/knowledge-center/knowledge/complete-cost-breakdown-for-house-projection-mapping.html)). Shared Nodes: **Visual Composition**, **Visual Content Library & Prep** (including codec choices — the proprietary-vs-open codec debate lives here: [Projectile Objects](https://projectileobjects.com/2025/11/28/resolume-vs-vdmx-vs-madmapper-vs-touchdesigner-which-live-visuals-software-and-why/)), **Projection & Display Basics**, **Signal Flow & Rig Setup**. A load-bearing gotcha connects the Paths: the standard VJ tool's cheaper tier cannot map — mapping, edge blending, and timecode are top-tier-only (€299 vs €799 — [Resolume support](https://resolume.com/support/en/avenue-arena-difference)). It is a different Path because the work is *site- and geometry-driven rather than beat-driven*: success is measured in alignment, blending, and reliability over hours, the client is an event producer or brand rather than a promoter, and much of the work is installation rather than performance.

**Event Technology (live sound, lighting, stage tech).** The crew-side craft that makes shows happen: PA and consoles, stage lighting and DMX control, power, rigging. Its canonical entry is not software but showing up — volunteer or work as a stagehand at venues, churches, and community events and learn on real rigs ([CareerExplorer](https://www.careerexplorer.com/careers/live-sound-engineer/education-and-training-requirements/)); free open-source lighting control ([QLC+](https://www.qlcplus.org/)) plus a cheap USB-DMX interface is the $0 practice rig. Shared Nodes: **Signal Flow & Rig Setup** (the single biggest overlap — "the most important skill you can have as a live sound engineer is a solid understanding of signal flow" — [Pro Audio Files](https://theproaudiofiles.com/complete-guide-basics-live-sound/)), **Equipment/Gear Access**, **Projection & Display Basics** (DMX also touches high-end VJ rigs), **Observing a Live Set**. It is a different Path because it is a service trade with a job ladder (stagehand → A2 → FOH), not a creative performance identity: the measure of success is the show going right and nobody noticing you, and "mixing is only 5% of the job" ([Mixing Music Live](https://www.mixingmusiclive.com/blog/mixing-is-only-5-of-the-job)). Structurally, it is the neighbor that stress-tests whether the domain model can represent apprenticeship-shaped routes.

**Music Production.** Composing, arranging, and mixing original tracks in a DAW — the most-trafficked neighbor, since "DJ/producer" is effectively one hyphenated career in dance music. Entry: pick one DAW and learn it deeply ($99–$749 tiers, with a real upgrade-economics split between lifetime-free-updates and paid major upgrades — [Image-Line](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio/pricing), [Audeobox](https://www.audeobox.com/learn/compare/ableton-pricing-guide/), [DJ TechTools on rent-to-own](https://djtechtools.com/2025/10/01/ableton-rent-to-own-not-subscription/)); the documented bridge from DJing is edits, intro versions, and simple remixes of tracks you already play ([Club Ready DJ School](https://www.clubreadydjschool.com/tribe-talk/getting-started/getting-started-for-producing-with-djs/)). Shared Nodes: **Rhythm & Song Structure** (a DJ's phrasing sense *is* arrangement literacy), **Music Theory Fundamentals** (core here, contested in DJ/VJ), **Harmonic Mixing / key knowledge**, **Music Selection & Library**. It is a different Path because it is a studio craft measured in finished tracks, not live performance: feedback arrives in release cycles rather than seconds, the deep skill tree (sound design, mixing) barely overlaps beatmatching or crowd-reading, and the payoff model is catalog and identity rather than gig fees. It also hosts a real produce-first vs DJ-first career debate ("very few DJs get booked without producing" — [Magnetic](https://magneticmag.com/2024/07/producers-vs-djs/) — vs "DJing pays this month" — [Techno Mag](https://www.technomag.fr/en/should-i-learn-to-dj-or-produce-first/)).

## 7. Artifacts, progress states, and selective sharing

*Fixture material for H5 (progress without pressure) and H6 (legible selective sharing), grounded in the milestones above.*

### Candidate Artifacts per milestone Node

| Node | Natural Artifact | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Private One-Track Experiment | a private recording of one blend, or a 30–60s screen capture of two visual changes landing on phrase | record-then-listen is the practice loop practitioners actually recommend ([Milkbar Nick](https://milkbarnick.substack.com/p/from-dj-nixperience-to-milkbar-nick)) |
| Mixing Technique | a recorded 30-minute practice mix | the classic self-assessment milestone, reachable in 4–8 sessions ([NaJade](https://djnajade.com/how-long-to-learn-to-dj/)) |
| Visual Content Library & Prep | the 60–90-second showreel | simultaneously a learning artifact and Guide B's gig currency ([chameleonic](https://chameleonic-design.com/top-5-vj-ing-tips/)) |

Evidence stays optional everywhere: every progress state below must be choosable with no Artifact attached (plan requirement).

### A worked example of the seven progress states

Applied to **Mixing Technique**: *interested* — bookmarked it, watched a tutorial; *tried* — one session on rented decks or free software, transitions mostly missed; *practicing* — regular sessions, blends landing at phrase boundaries more often than not; *demonstrated* — recorded a clean 30-minute mix or played the checkpoint set, optionally attaching the recording as evidence; *paused* — no booth access this term, intending to return; *skipped* — went straight to the sync workflow without manual beatmatching, a legitimate documented route (the sync debate, §1); *not for me* — decided performing music live isn't the appeal and kept the visual craft. None of these carries a deadline, streak, or grade — and the research shows *skipped* and *not for me* are respectable real-world outcomes (sync-first DJs; VJs who never touch audio).

### What a plausible share page contains — and what stays private

A realistic selective share for an Explorer on Guide A pitching for an open-decks slot: chosen interests (Music + Technology), the adopted Guide with source attribution, the Build's *practicing*/*demonstrated* Steps for Mixing Technique and Club Media-Player Workflow, and one Artifact — the recorded mix. Private unless separately chosen: progress states on every other Node (especially *not for me* and *paused*), the keyboard-only remix history, all other Artifacts, and any other interests. The Guide B equivalent shares the showreel plus a *demonstrated* Reactive Visuals Step — and nothing about music Nodes at all. That asymmetry is the point: the share page is a pitch surface, and both crafts already run on curated pitch artifacts (the USB in the pocket, the reel-first booking).

No fixture should use "gig-ready in 30 days" framing — practitioners call it out.

## 9. Open questions

Things a schema designer should know before freezing the fixture model:

1. **"Excluded" is not in the canonical role vocabulary.** The prototype plan requires theory/instrument to be markable "required, recommended, optional, or excluded," but [vocabulary.md](../vocabulary.md) defines only required / recommended / optional depth / alternative / checkpoint. This report additionally needed **absent** (never placed) as distinct from **excluded** (deliberately left out, with rationale — Guide B's theory stance). Decide whether exclusion is a role, a Guide-level annotation, or simply an explanation attached to a not-placed Node; the fixture currently needs the distinction to render the disagreement.
2. **Alternatives are branches, not single Steps.** The "alternative" role attaches to a Node, but real alternatives in the research are multi-node sub-routes (Guide A's three-way gear-access branch; Guide B's DJ-technique branch implies mixing technique *plus* music selection *plus* possibly harmonic mixing). The schema should clarify whether an alternative can carry its own ordered children, or the fixture will flatten something practitioners treat as a route.
3. **Node granularity has no principled floor yet.** The research supports splitting Reactive Visuals into 2–3 nodes (BPM sync vs FFT vs phrase-aware performance, per Resolume's own training units) and splitting Mixing Technique into 3–4 (beatmatching, EQ/gain, transitions, phrasing already separate). This report merged toward the plan's ~16-node cap (landing at seventeen); the sync-ladder research (tap tempo → FFT → MIDI → OSC → Link → timecode) shows a whole strand compressed into two nodes. If Creator sessions show practitioners fighting the granularity (research-plan risk "recognize where the model oversimplifies"), the Atlas needs a split/merge story, not just new-node proposal review.
4. **Software is deliberately not a Node — but Resource nodes are underspecified.** Per the rules, brand names live in descriptions/resources. Yet real route decisions are tool-shaped (Resolume-first vs TouchDesigner-first is disagreement #6 in the curricula research; Avenue-vs-Arena is a load-bearing cost gate). Vocabulary allows Resource Nodes "when it materially affects a route" — the fixture needs a convention for when a tool crosses that line, or Guides will smuggle tools into Skill-node descriptions inconsistently.
5. **Career/business content is a parallel strand, not a terminal Step.** Every commercial curriculum threads getting-gigs/promotion alongside skills rather than after them (Digital DJ Tips "Playing Out → Promoting", Crossfader's gigs course, VJ Academy's reel-first playbook). A strictly ordered route model may misrepresent this; consider whether Guides can mark a strand as running in parallel.
6. **Role assignments are genre- and context-dependent.** Plastician's documented point — key mixing essential for melodic genres, nearly irrelevant for chop-and-drop — means the *same author* could honestly assign different roles per genre. The current model locates roles per Guide; that's workable (one Guide per context) but worth stating explicitly, since Creator-session participants may push back.
7. **Cost/currency facts decay fast.** Verified facts in this report already carry churn: 2025 US tariff price drift, the Mar 2026 Beatport/Beatsource merger, Twitch subsidy step-down through ~Jul 2025, a dead VJ tool (CoGe) that stale guides still recommend, a repo migration (Strudel → Codeberg). Fixture content should carry as-of dates, and the schema should decide where dated realities live (Node? Guide note? separate realities layer, as this appendix assumes).
8. **Research thin spots.** (a) Direct Reddit threads (r/DJs, r/Beatmatch, r/vjing) were unreachable; community consensus is paraphrased from secondary sources and must not be quoted verbatim in fixtures. (b) Several 2026 guide sites (vjacademy.info, renderwave.io, djmirror.com) are of uncertain editorial depth and were used only where corroborated — fixture prose citing their unique numbers (e.g., VJ day rates, cost tiers) should hedge. (c) Streaming-platform share figures are single-source and indicative only. (d) No primary research on hybrid AV acts at beginner level exists beyond a handful of practitioner accounts (Dijkstra, AJ Johnson) — the "ten-minute AV set" anatomy is a synthesis, clearly labeled as such above.
9. **Checkpoint vs Milestone overlap.** The Ten-Minute AV Set is a Milestone-type Node that both Guides use as (or near) a checkpoint, while Guide B checkpoints the one-track experiment instead. The model handles this (checkpoint is a route role, Milestone a node type), but Explorer sessions should verify people don't read "checkpoint" as an exam — the research plan's no-grading criterion is sensitive exactly here, and Scratch DJ Academy's *assessed* 10-minute set shows the real world does grade it.
10. **Guide-level stance metadata.** The most valuable authorial content surfaced by this research is positions in named debates (sync vs manual, automation vs performed, stock vs original content, theory vs ears). Currently that lives in free-text rationale. If Guide comparison (H2, "at least three material differences") should surface disagreements mechanically, consider whether stances deserve structure — or whether prose rationale plus differing role assignments is enough for the prototype.
