# Explorer and Creator Flows

- **Status:** Canonical journey direction
- **Last updated:** 2026-08-15

The product has two connected workflows. Neither side is complete without the
other: Explorers need credible routes, and Creators need a way to turn
experience into useful, testable roadmaps.

## Explorer journey

### 1. Enter from curiosity

The Explorer can begin with any of the following:

- selected interests or strengths;
- a direct search for a Path;
- a shared Guide or Galaxy link;
- a broad prompt such as "show me possibilities";
- no confident interests yet.

Input stays light. The product does not require a personality test or complete
life audit before showing value.

### 2. Discover Paths

The Universe reveals a small, understandable set of possible directions. Each
result explains why it appeared without claiming certainty.

The Explorer can browse beyond recommendations. Personalization helps
orientation but never hides the larger possibility space.

### 3. Understand one Path

The Path overview explains what people do, common outcomes, environments,
costs, time patterns, safety considerations, and major variants.

The Explorer should be able to answer, "What is this actually like?" before
choosing a roadmap.

### 4. Compare Guides

The Explorer compares a small set of routes through the Path. Differences are
made explicit:

- intended audience and starting point;
- required, recommended, optional, and alternative Steps;
- time, cost, equipment, and setting assumptions;
- expected outcome;
- author context and disclosures;
- version, freshness, and attempt-based feedback.

Popularity alone is not treated as quality.

### 5. Adopt or remix a Journey

The Explorer can use a Guide as written, combine parts of several Guides, or
change the route before starting. The result is a personal Journey.

The application explains provenance without implying that the Creator controls
the personal copy.

### 6. Explore at their own pace

The Journey shows possible next Steps without becoming an obligation list. The
Explorer may learn, observe, try a Quest, practice, attach an Artifact, jump to
another branch, or pause.

Progress language reflects what actually happened. Trying a Step does not
claim mastery, and "not for me" does not count as failure.

### 7. Reorient

Progress changes route emphasis and nearby possibilities. The Explorer remains
in control of any proposed change and can reject or edit it.

### 8. Share selectively

The Explorer may create a read-only view for a friend, mentor, community, or
job application. They select individual Paths, Steps, and Artifacts. Private
notes and unrelated activity remain hidden.

### Parent-facilitated variation

A parent may help a child enter interests, inspect unfamiliar Paths, compare
age-appropriate Guides, and arrange access to a real-world Step. The child can
confirm or reject the suggested signals, choose the Journey, use "not for me," and
participate in every sharing decision.

The parent is a facilitator, not the owner of the child's identity or progress.
A future family implementation must make consent, visibility, evidence access,
and age-appropriate safety explicit. Until that implementation exists, parent
and child can co-view an adult-owned or non-persisted exploration session.

## Creator journey

### 1. Choose the destination and audience

The Creator selects an existing Path or drafts a Path proposal. They state who
the Guide is for, the assumed starting point, and the observable outcome.

### 2. Assemble the route

The Creator searches shared Nodes and places them on a branching roadmap. They
mark each Step as required, recommended, optional depth, alternative, or
checkpoint.

If a concept is missing, they create a provisional custom Node rather than a
silent duplicate.

### 3. Explain the route

The Creator adds the practical context that makes advice trustworthy:

- why each meaningful Step is included;
- time, cost, equipment, location, and access assumptions;
- safety and age considerations;
- where alternatives work;
- what the route does not cover;
- relevant experience and disclosures.

### 4. Preview as an Explorer

Before sharing, the Creator sees the same Guide comparison and Journey adoption
view an Explorer will see. Missing outcomes, unexplained hard prerequisites,
and disconnected Nodes are surfaced.

### 5. Share at the appropriate level

The Creator can:

- keep the Guide private;
- create an unlisted link;
- invite collaborators;
- submit it for public Path discovery.

Public submission is not automatic publication.

### 6. Learn from attempts

The Creator sees structured, privacy-preserving feedback from people who
actually used the Guide. Saves and page views are separated from attempts,
helpfulness, accuracy, and needs-update reports.

### 7. Version rather than silently rewrite

Material changes produce a new Guide version and a plain-language change log.
Existing personal Journeys are offered the update; they are not overwritten.

## Connected DJ/VJ golden journey

### Creator side

An experienced DJ/VJ creates **Club-first DJ/VJ with borrowed gear** for music
fans who are technically curious but new to live visuals.

The Creator reuses shared Nodes such as rhythm, music selection, signal flow,
visual composition, reactive visuals, and live control. Music theory and
learning an instrument are marked as optional depth rather than universal
prerequisites. The Guide ends with a private ten-minute audiovisual set.

The Creator shares an unlisted link with an adult tester.

### Explorer side

The Explorer opens the Guide, sees its author and assumptions, and compares it
with a visual-first alternative. They adopt the club-first route but replace
the controller Step with a keyboard-only alternative.

They try one bounded Step: synchronize two visual changes with sections of a
familiar track. They record the Step as tried, keep a private note, and leave
the next Step unscheduled.

The Universe now shows the personal Journey and related possibilities without a
streak, deadline, or global level. The Explorer may later share only the Journey
title, selected interests, the tried Step, and a chosen Artifact.

## Flow acceptance questions

An Explorer should be able to explain:

1. the difference between a Path, Guide, and personal Journey;
2. why two credible Guides can disagree;
3. which parts of the Journey they can change;
4. what their progress state does and does not claim;
5. what will remain private if they share a profile.

A Creator should be able to explain:

1. when to reuse a Node and when to propose a new one;
2. the difference between required and recommended;
3. why their experience does not make their route universal;
4. what happens when they publish a new version;
5. which feedback comes from actual attempts.
