# Life Leveling Product Thesis

- **Status:** Canonical
- **Last updated:** 2026-08-15

## One-sentence promise

Life Leveling is a community-powered Universe of hobbies, skills, and possible
life directions where people can discover roadmaps, follow or remix routes
created by practitioners, and optionally document what they explore.

The useful shorthand is **roadmap.sh for life paths, with community routes and
personal progress**. Life paths include creative practices, hobbies, crafts,
fields of study, projects, and careers; the product is not limited to jobs.

## Problem

People often recognize fragments of themselves - interests, strengths,
curiosities, or activities they enjoy - without knowing what those fragments
could become or how to explore them. The available information is fragmented:

- search results explain isolated topics rather than coherent routes;
- career tools jump too quickly to job titles and permanent identity;
- course platforms optimize for enrollment rather than orientation;
- social media displays polished outcomes without realistic starting points;
- practitioners hold valuable route knowledge that is difficult to structure,
  compare, update, or pass on;
- trackers assume the person already knows what should be pursued.

Life Leveling begins one step earlier. It helps a person see plausible
directions, understand several routes into them, and create a roadmap they own.

## Core value

The primary value is **orientation through understandable roadmaps**.

Real-world Steps, reflection, evidence, and progress tracking support that
value. They are not the product's sole identity and must not turn exploration
into another obligation system.

The value hierarchy is:

1. **See possibilities.** Discover Paths that connect interests or answer a
   curiosity.
2. **Understand routes.** Compare how experienced people approach the same
   Path, including disagreements and trade-offs.
3. **Take ownership.** Adopt, change, combine, or create a personal Journey.
4. **Explore without pressure.** Try Steps, skip them, pause, or change
   direction without punishment.
5. **Remember and demonstrate selectively.** Keep private progress or share a
   deliberate view of interests, work, and demonstrated outcomes.
6. **Contribute knowledge.** Turn practitioner experience into a reusable,
   versioned Guide for other people.

## Who it serves

### Explorer

Someone who wants to understand what is possible and how to begin. They may
have clear interests, a specific destination, or no confident direction yet.

Examples include:

- a technically minded music fan exploring DJ/VJ practice;
- an adult looking for a new hobby or side project;
- a learner comparing several routes into a field;
- a parent and child exploring possibilities together;
- a candidate choosing which interests or demonstrated projects to share.

### Creator

A practitioner, teacher, mentor, or experienced learner who wants to make their
route knowledge useful to other people. A Creator can assemble shared concepts
into a Guide, explain assumptions and alternatives, and update it as the field
changes.

One person may be an Explorer on one Path and a Creator on another.

## Product shape

The Universe is a shared map of Paths and reusable concepts. A Path describes a
destination such as DJ/VJ, sports storytelling, or furniture making. It may
contain an official orientation and many community-authored Guides.

A Guide is not universal truth. It is an author's reasoned route through the
Path for a stated audience and context. An Explorer adopts or remixes a Guide
into a personal Journey. Their Journey remains theirs even if the source Guide is
updated, hidden, or replaced.

Creators may present a themed collection as a Galaxy, but shared concepts keep
their stable identity underneath. This allows different Guides to reuse music
theory, signal flow, visual composition, or live performance without creating
isolated duplicates.

## Product principles

### A map, not a task manager

The application shows directions and relationships. It does not manufacture a
daily obligation queue.

### Routes are opinions with context

Several credible routes can lead toward the same Path. Requirements,
recommendations, optional depth, and alternatives must be distinguishable.

### The Explorer owns the Journey

Recommendations can suggest; only the person decides what to adopt, skip,
reorder, pause, or share.

### Lived progress is more meaningful than app activity

Opening screens, maintaining a streak, or checking arbitrary boxes is not
mastery. Trying, practicing, making, helping, performing, and demonstrating are
meaningful when the person says they are.

### No punishment for curiosity

There are no broken-streak penalties, overdue shame, expiring rewards, or
global life levels. Pausing and deciding "not for me" are legitimate map states.

### Private by default, selectively shareable

Interests, notes, evidence, and progress belong to the person. A public profile
or portfolio is an explicit, granular projection - never a mirror of all
private activity.

### Trust is structural

Guide authors disclose audience, assumptions, experience, cost, time,
equipment, safety considerations, version, and last review. Attempt-based
feedback matters more than saves or popularity.

### Children retain agency

A parent can help a child explore, but must not assign a permanent identity or
publish the child's activity by default. Child accounts require a dedicated
consent, privacy, safety, and age-appropriate design phase. The first roadmap
prototype uses adults.

## Success

The primary product outcome is:

> A person finds a plausible Path, understands why different routes exist, and
> adopts or remixes a roadmap that feels like their own.

The strongest downstream signal is meaningful progress on that Journey: a Step
tried, a capability practiced, a project demonstrated, or an informed decision
to pause or leave the Path.

Healthy early measures include:

- Paths discovered that the Explorer did not already know;
- Guides meaningfully compared before adoption;
- Journeys adopted and deliberately changed rather than passively accepted;
- Explorers who return because the next possibility is useful, not because a
  streak demands it;
- progress updates the Explorer describes as accurate and non-judgmental;
- selectively shared profiles that distinguish interest from demonstrated
  capability;
- Guides improved after credible attempt feedback.

Time in app, daily active use, raw checklist completion, and streak length are
not primary success measures.

## Product boundary

The near-term product is roadmap discovery, authoring, adoption, remixing,
personal progress, and selective sharing.

It is not initially:

- a general habit tracker or productivity system;
- a universal score for a person's life;
- an automated career oracle;
- an open, unmoderated marketplace;
- a social feed optimized for attention;
- a child-monitoring or parental surveillance product;
- a credential issuer claiming objective mastery;
- a replacement for formal education, medical advice, or professional safety
  training.
