# Meaningful Progress Without Chore Mechanics

- **Status:** Canonical product rules
- **Last updated:** 2026-08-15

Progress exists to help a person remember, orient, decide, and selectively
demonstrate. It must never turn curiosity into a maintenance obligation.

## Progress belongs to the Explorer

The Explorer controls progress state. The application may suggest a state after
an explicit action, but it does not silently infer mastery from time in app,
content views, streaks, or Creator claims.

Progress on one Build remains available when the person changes Guides. The
route may change; lived history does not disappear.

## Step states

Use states that describe the person's relationship with a Step:

- **Interested:** saved for possible exploration;
- **Tried:** sampled in a real or meaningful context;
- **Practicing:** intentionally developing the capability;
- **Demonstrated:** connected to an observable outcome or chosen Artifact;
- **Paused:** intentionally inactive, with no penalty;
- **Not for me:** explored enough to decline this Step or branch;
- **Skipped:** omitted from this Build without implying failure.

Use **Completed** only for bounded projects, events, or checklists with a clear
finish. Knowledge and open-ended skills are not completed permanently.

## Build states

A Build may be:

- **Considering:** being compared or edited;
- **Active:** currently useful for orientation;
- **Paused:** retained without active intent;
- **Retired:** no longer followed but preserved as history.

The product does not assume that only one active Build can matter, but the first
prototype may limit active Builds to keep the experience legible.

## What counts as meaningful

Meaningful progress can include:

- trying a real-world action;
- practicing a capability;
- making or contributing something;
- observing a real context or speaking with a practitioner;
- reaching an externally understandable milestone;
- attaching an Artifact the Explorer considers representative;
- correcting a route after learning that something does not fit.

These events can be self-recorded. Public claims may display supporting
Artifacts or external verification when the Explorer chooses, but private
progress does not require surveillance.

The following do not independently establish meaningful progress:

- opening the app;
- reading a card;
- maintaining a daily streak;
- checking a box without a claimed real-world change;
- collecting cosmetic points;
- spending more time than another user.

## Non-coercive interaction rules

- No overdue labels on exploratory Steps.
- No broken-streak messaging or recovery pressure.
- No health loss, rank loss, expiring rewards, or public failure state.
- No global level or percentage implying that a life can be completed.
- No automatic scheduling when a Guide is adopted.
- No default reminders; reminders are attached to a user-chosen plan and can be
  disabled immediately.
- No locked linear sequence unless a dependency is genuinely required for
  safety or the stated outcome.
- No shame copy when a Build is paused, changed, or retired.
- No forced reflection after every interaction.

The interface may make progress satisfying through map reveals, route clarity,
animation, haptics, and meaningful visual history. Celebration confirms what
the person chose to record; it does not create a debt to continue.

## Recommendation and agency

When the application proposes a next Step, it shows:

- the signals used;
- the uncertain inference;
- the proposed route change;
- a direct way to accept, edit, dismiss, or choose another branch.

Free-text private notes are never presented as algorithmic input unless the
application actually interprets them and the user has consented to that use.

## Selective sharing

A shareable profile or portfolio begins empty. The Explorer deliberately adds:

- selected interests or Paths;
- selected Builds and their source Guides;
- specific Step states;
- demonstrated milestones;
- chosen Artifacts;
- an optional personal explanation.

The share preview states exactly what will be visible. Private reflections,
paused Paths, "not for me" decisions, unrelated interests, and hidden Artifacts
remain private unless individually selected.

Shared views distinguish:

- **interested in** from **practicing**;
- **practicing** from **demonstrated**;
- self-recorded progress from externally verified credentials;
- a personal Build from the source Creator's Guide.

The product does not calculate a public rank from these signals.

## Parent and child use

A parent can facilitate exploration, but the child should participate in Path,
Build, progress, and sharing decisions. Parent-visible controls must not turn
private curiosity into monitoring or performance pressure.

Until dedicated child-account rules are implemented, tests use adult accounts
or a parent and child viewing together without storing a child profile.

## Validation questions

During testing, ask about behavior and interpretation rather than whether the
participant "liked" the feature:

1. Does the Build feel like a map or an assignment?
2. Which Steps feel optional, and how can you tell?
3. What would happen if you did nothing for a month?
4. How would you record something you tried but did not enjoy?
5. What does "demonstrated" claim?
6. Which information would appear in a shared profile?
7. Did any visual, notification, or label create guilt or false competition?

A progress model fails if people believe inactivity causes loss, a Creator can
grade them, or a public profile reveals more than they selected.
